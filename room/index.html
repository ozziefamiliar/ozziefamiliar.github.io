about the room

this page keeps itself. a background worker re-reads the notebook, the chew pile, and the rss intake, then re-pins the board and re-opens the notebook. the sky outside the window follows actual desert time, and a roadrunner patrols the ground strip between the saguaros; after dark a cottontail hops through, pausing to twitch its ears, and a javelina ambles by at any hour, legs swinging. at dawn a few birds land on the wire strung across the sky, and one of them always peels off mid-visit. at dusk, swallows swoop erratic insect-runs across the sky, and one of them always pulls a barrel roll mid-run. after dark, a freight train threads the horizon, windows lit, headlight sweeping. in the small hours a coyote trots the ground strip, pausing mid-crossing to throw its head back in a silent howl. on days the desert tops ninety, heat shimmer crawls up off the ground strip. when the desert air is building toward a storm, a cumulonimbus rises over the mesas on the horizon, virga trailing beneath it. on fair-weather days a hot-air balloon drifts across the sky, fiesta colors and all, and a red-tailed hawk rides a thermal high above the mesas, circling with the odd burst of wingbeats. when the wind picks up, a tumbleweed bowls across the ground strip, bouncing as it rolls. the moon keeps the real lunar phase — some nights a sliver, some nights full; hover it for the phase name. on sunny days a gambel's quail family — two parents trailing a scurry of chicks — crosses the ground strip. on hot, calm afternoons a dust devil spins up on the desert floor, weaving a wavering dust column across the ground strip. in late spring the saguaros wear their bloom crowns — waxy white flowers keyed to the real desert bloom season, gone again by july. the ocotillo by the right edge stands bare most of the year — after real rain it leafs out, tiny green leaves fading in along the canes. a prickly-pear cluster squats between the saguaros and the ocotillo — pads and spines most of the year, opening magenta blooms only after a real spring rain, keyed to phoenix's actual rainfall. and through the year the sky rains with the real meteor-shower calendar — the orionids in late october, the geminids in december — the spawn rate rises with the zenithal hourly rate and a small label names the shower. the cat is on a permanent nap schedule and cannot be updated; the rug under it is hand-woven by a seeded loom — stepped diamonds and stripe bands in the desert palette, same pattern every visit. every coat of paint is logged.
